## [symptoms]

- Persistent knee pain that wakes you at night or limits walking more than 500 metres
- Stiffness in the morning lasting more than 30 minutes
- Clear bone-on-bone changes on X-ray, or a knee that gives way under load
- Pain that no longer responds to physiotherapy, weight loss, or anti-inflammatory medication
- Significant deformity (bow-legged or knock-kneed) developing over time

## [options]

- Non-surgical first-line: Weight management, targeted physiotherapy, and activity modification resolve many cases of early arthritis.
- Medical management: Short-course anti-inflammatories, selective intra-articular injections (hyaluronic acid or corticosteroid) where appropriate.
- Partial (uni-compartmental) knee replacement: For damage isolated to one compartment — smaller incision, faster recovery, preserves more of your natural knee.
- Total knee replacement: Gold standard for widespread arthritis. Replaces all three compartments with a precision-engineered implant.
- Robotic-assisted knee replacement: Uses real-time imaging and robotic guidance for sub-millimeter implant placement. Particularly valuable in complex anatomy or revision surgery.

## [what-to-expect]

- Pre-operative assessment including X-rays, blood work, and medical clearance. Most patients attend a single pre-op consultation.
- Spinal or general anaesthesia selected with the anaesthetist. Most knees are done under spinal, which speeds early recovery.
- The damaged joint surfaces are resurfaced with metal and polyethylene components. Incision is typically 10–12 cm.
- Average theatre time is 60–90 minutes. Blood loss with robotic-assisted technique is usually under 100 ml.
- Hospital stay of 2–4 days. Walking with a frame begins within 24 hours.

## [recovery]

- Day 1: Stand and walk a few steps with a frame. Pain controlled with multimodal medication.
- Week 1: Discharged home on crutches or walker. Daily physiotherapy begins.
- Weeks 2–6: Progressive weight bearing, stair climbing, transition off walking aids.
- Month 3: Most patients are walking freely, driving, and resuming sedentary work.
- Month 6 and beyond: Full strength returns. Implants, when well-placed, last 20–25 years.

## [risks]

- Infection (under 1 percent with modern protocols, but serious when it occurs)
- Blood clots in the leg or lung — reduced with early mobilisation and blood thinners
- Stiffness or limited bend in a small minority of patients
- Implant loosening over decades (revision surgery may be needed eventually)
- Nerve or blood vessel injury — very rare with experienced surgeons

## [faqs]

### How do I know if I need a knee replacement?

When knee pain limits daily activities despite 6+ months of conservative care and imaging shows significant joint damage, replacement is worth discussing. We never recommend surgery as a first step.

### What is the difference between robotic and traditional knee replacement?

Robotic-assisted surgery uses 3D imaging and robotic guidance to place implants within sub-millimeter accuracy. The surgeon controls every cut and movement — the robot is a precision tool. Studies show better implant positioning, which correlates with longer implant life.

### How long until I can walk normally?

Most patients walk with a frame on day 1, graduate to a single crutch in 2–3 weeks, and walk unaided between weeks 4 and 6. Stair climbing and outdoor walking usually resume by week 6.

### Will my knee feel normal again?

Most patients describe the replaced knee as "better than the arthritic knee" but slightly different from a natural knee. Kneeling can feel odd for some. Pain relief is usually dramatic and lasting.

### Is the surgery covered by insurance?

Most Indian health insurance plans cover both traditional and robotic knee replacement. The clinic team verifies coverage and obtains approvals before scheduling.

### How long do modern knee implants last?

Current-generation implants, placed accurately, last 20–25 years in the majority of patients. Robotic precision contributes to longer implant life because a well-aligned implant wears more evenly.

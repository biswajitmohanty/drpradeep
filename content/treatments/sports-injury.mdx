## [symptoms]

- A knee or shoulder that "gave way" during a twist, tackle, or fall
- Ongoing swelling, clicking, or locking of a joint
- Weakness or instability that's limiting your sport or daily activity
- Pain that hasn't settled with 4–6 weeks of rest and physiotherapy
- A dislocated shoulder or kneecap that keeps popping out

## [options]

- Rest, rehabilitation, and gradual return-to-sport: The first approach for most overuse injuries and partial tears.
- Injection therapy: Corticosteroid or hyaluronic acid injections for specific tendinopathies and early cartilage problems.
- Arthroscopic ligament reconstruction: Keyhole surgery for ACL, PCL, or multi-ligament injuries — using the patient's own tendon.
- Arthroscopic meniscus repair or partial removal: Preserves as much healthy meniscus as possible to protect the knee long-term.
- Shoulder stabilisation (Bankart repair or Latarjet): For recurrent dislocations — returns athletes to contact sport.

## [what-to-expect]

- Clinical examination, targeted MRI, and a conversation about your sport and goals.
- Most sports surgery is done as a day-case or overnight stay.
- General or regional anaesthesia depending on the joint.
- Arthroscopic technique means 2–3 small incisions, minimal scarring, and faster recovery than open surgery.
- A detailed rehabilitation plan is shared with your physiotherapist the same week.

## [recovery]

- Week 1–2: Pain and swelling control, simple range-of-motion exercises.
- Week 2–6: Progressive strengthening, careful reintroduction of load.
- Month 3: Sport-specific drills begin (running, cutting, throwing — depending on the sport).
- Month 6–9: Graduated return to competitive sport for major ligament reconstructions, with objective testing before full return.
- Ongoing: Strength and movement screening to prevent re-injury.

## [risks]

- Re-injury if return to sport is rushed (the single biggest predictor of failure)
- Stiffness in the early weeks if rehab is not followed
- Graft failure in ACL reconstruction (2–5 percent)
- Infection (under 1 percent with arthroscopic technique)
- Residual instability requiring revision surgery in rare cases

## [faqs]

### Do all ACL tears need surgery?

No. Sedentary patients or those who do low-pivot sports may manage well without surgery. Reconstruction is recommended when the knee feels unstable or when you want to return to cutting sports.

### How long after ACL surgery can I play football or cricket again?

Typically 9–12 months, with a structured rehab programme and objective return-to-sport testing. Rushing back earlier increases re-injury risk significantly.

### Can a torn meniscus heal without surgery?

Small tears in certain locations can. Larger or symptomatic tears usually need arthroscopic repair or partial removal. We try to repair rather than remove whenever possible.

### What is a PRP injection and does it work?

Platelet-rich plasma involves injecting concentrated platelets from your own blood into an injured tissue. Evidence is strongest for certain tendinopathies (tennis elbow, patellar tendon) — less clear for meniscus or ACL problems. We only offer PRP where the evidence supports it.

### Is shoulder surgery painful?

Shoulder surgery is often the most uncomfortable in the first 48 hours. A regional anaesthetic block and multimodal painkillers keep things well-controlled. Most patients sleep in a recliner or with a pillow for the first 1–2 weeks.

### Will I be as good as before?

For well-selected patients with a structured rehab programme, most return to their previous level of sport. A few come back stronger because of improved movement and strength training during rehab.
